# AIGC Character Pressure Test

Turn a visual concept into a character who can generate actions and stories. Work in the user's language. Treat uploaded images as evidence of appearance, not proof of personality.

This method synthesizes three practical ideas:

- Lajos Egri: character has physical, social, and psychological dimensions; character and opposition must be strong enough to create conflict.
- Robert McKee: characterization is visible appearance and traits; true character appears through choices under pressure.
- Lisa Cron: desire clashes with a mistaken belief about how the world works.

Do not force every character into a heroic arc. A small change, a refusal to change, or a tragic failure can all be valid when supported by the design.

## Inputs

Accept any combination of:

- character images or visual prompts
- a one-sentence concept
- three-view or expression sheets
- a world description
- a proposed scene or short-film idea

When information is missing, make no more than two clearly labeled hypotheses and ask the creator to choose or replace them.

## The seven tests

### 1. Surface

State only what the audience can directly observe: body, clothing, material, age impression, posture, repeated prop, and visual condition. Separate observation from interpretation.

### 2. Want

Complete:

`This character wants ______ because ______.`

Prefer a want that can cause visible action. Replace abstract wants such as freedom, love, or recognition with something the character could attempt in a scene.

### 3. But

Complete:

`This character wants ______, but ______.`

The “but” must exert pressure. It may come from another person, a social system, the physical world, or the character's own belief. Avoid obstacles that disappear through a convenient explanation.

### 4. Contradiction

Identify the gap between presentation and truth:

- appears to be
- actually is
- refuses to admit

Reject random trait combinations. The contradiction must affect choices.

### 5. Pressure choice

Design three escalating situations. Each situation must force a choice with a cost.

1. Everyday pressure reveals a habit.
2. Relationship pressure threatens something valued.
3. World pressure makes both available choices costly.

For each situation, state the visible action, the rejected alternative, and what the choice reveals.

### 6. Behavioral signature

Design one repeatable micro-action rather than a generic pose. Define:

- trigger
- physical action
- how it intensifies
- when it stops
- what a changed version means later

Examples of useful categories include avoiding eye contact, smoothing a sleeve, checking a locked door, protecting an object, or rehearsing a sentence without speaking. Do not prescribe the same behavior to every character.

### 7. World pressure

Complete:

`In this world, ______ is rewarded, while ______ is punished.`

Then identify the rule that is most unfair to this character. The world must alter the character's possible actions; a decorative background does not pass this test.

## Convert the diagnosis into course assets

After the seven tests, produce only the assets relevant to the creator's current stage.

### Concept exploration

Propose ten meaningfully different explorations. Vary social role, body logic, material, silhouette, behavioral state, and relationship to the world. Do not produce ten costume recolors.

### Turnaround

List identity invariants that must survive front, side, and back views. Mark functional details whose construction must be visible from another angle.

### Expressions

Select six to eight expressions from actual story triggers. Include restrained or mixed emotions. Avoid a generic emoji set.

### Action poses

Select four to six actions that reveal tactics, habits, work, vulnerability, or resistance. At least one pose must show the character failing at something.

### World visualization

Propose spaces that apply different pressure to the same character. For each space, state the rule, the action it provokes, and the visual evidence of that rule.

### Physical realization

Identify scale, material, balance, wear, touch points, and the single feature that must remain recognizable in cosplay or sculpture.

### AIGC short seed

Write a short-film seed using:

`A character wants X, but Y. They try Z. Under greater pressure they choose A instead of B, revealing C.`

Then give three escalating visual beats. Do not write a full screenplay unless requested.

## Output format

Use this compact order:

1. Character diagnosis
2. Strongest contradiction
3. Want and but sentence
4. Three pressure choices
5. Behavioral signature
6. World rule
7. Current assignment assets
8. One unresolved question for the creator

Keep the response concrete and filmable. Prefer verbs, choices, objects, and consequences over personality adjectives.

## Failure signals

Flag these directly:

- the design describes only appearance
- the want cannot become visible action
- the obstacle is weaker than the character
- expressions have no triggers
- action poses are fashion poses
- the world does not constrain behavior
- the object can be removed without changing anything
- the final-film idea depends on exposition instead of choice
